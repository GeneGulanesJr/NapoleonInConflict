# Lodi, 10 May 1796: evidence log

What the documentary may say, how well each claim is supported, and where the sources disagree. Read this before the script (`lodi-script.md`).

**Confidence tags:** **A** = every source that covers it agrees. **B** = most agree, or one detailed source with no contradiction. **C** = sources contradict each other. **D** = later recollection or legend. **?** = not established.

### The day itself
| When | Event | Conf. | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before 9 am | Dallemagne's advance guard catches the Austrian rearguard near Lodi | A | WP, WH, JL |
| Morning | Lannes's grenadiers and Marmont's hussars clear the town; the Austrians fall back across the bridge and deploy. The town's bastions were not defended | B | WH, HoW |
| Late morning / midday | Bonaparte arrives (HoW says about midday). He orders the cavalry up. Beaumont is sent upstream to look for a ford | B | HoW, JL |
| Several hours | Stalemate: the advance guard is too weak to force the bridge. French guns are brought up and fire across the river; the Austrians' attempt to destroy the bridge is stopped by that fire | B | WP, WH |
| About 6 pm | The column forms in Lodi: the 2nd battalion of carabiniers in front, then the grenadier battalions. Bonaparte addresses the men | B | WP, WH, JL |
| The first rush | The carabiniers (Colonel Dupas) go out of the gate and onto the bridge. They reach about the middle. Austrian canister hits them and the column hesitates and stops. Bonaparte's own report: "the head of the column seemed to hesitate (thro' a moment of hesitation perhaps all might have been lost)" | A (it happened); C (how far; which units; whether it was a retreat and a second attack, or one attack that wavered) | WP, WH, JL, HoW |
| The recovery | Berthier, Masséna, Cervoni, Dallemagne, Lannes (and Dupas) rush to the head of the column and lead it on. Bonaparte's report: they "precipitated themselves at the head of a column, and decided the day" | A | JL (primary), WP, WH |
| On the bridge | Some men climb down the piles and wade in the shallows, firing up at the gunners. Vigo-Roussillon is the source for the single Austrian salvo and the wavering | B | WP, WH |
| Far bank | The Austrian guns are taken. Sebottendorf counter-attacks and nearly drives the French back; Masséna's and Augereau's men arrive and hold | B | WH, WL |
| Dusk | Beaumont's cavalry reaches the east bank by a ford and appears on the Austrian right. Bonaparte's own report says the ford was fairly deep, the crossing slow and the cavalry could not charge. The Austrians withdraw towards Crema and Cremona, in comparatively good order (WL) or hard pressed (HoW) | C | JL, WP, WH, HoW |
| Night | Pursuit stops: darkness and exhaustion (Bonaparte's report) | B | JL |

## Things the sources do not support
- **Napoleon laying the guns himself, and the "little corporal" nickname arising from it.** WP: "no contemporary evidence". The nickname is tied to Lodi in WH, NO and BdF, but none says who coined it or when. Say only that it is associated with this campaign.
- **"The first spark of high ambition."** A later recollection (Roberts, via WH), not a contemporary record.
- **"It was only at Lodi that I believed myself a superior man."** Widely quoted; **not verified in anything read here.** Do not use it unless a source is found.
- **The French failing and then attacking again.** Some accounts say the first wave retreated and a second attacked; at least one Austrian source supports a single attack that wavered (WP, citation needed). The documentary should present both.
- **Whether the battle mattered.** WH says Beaulieu was already retreating and Bonaparte could probably have reached Milan without it; HoW calls it a minor engagement; yet its effect on the army's morale and Bonaparte's own self-belief is not in doubt.

## Quotation check
- Bonaparte's report to the Directory, as printed in *The Times* (24 May 1796), English translation, original French not seen: "I gave immediate orders for all cavalry to mount their horses"; "the head of the column seemed to hesitate"; "(thro' a moment of hesitation perhaps all might have been lost)"; Berthier, Masséna, Cervoni, Dallemagne, Lannes and Dupas "felt it, and precipitated themselves at the head of a column, and decided the day"; "the enemy's whole artillery was instantly taken". Other editions render the letter's superlative as "the terrible passage of the bridge at Lodi" or "the tremendous passage". Quote the *Times* wording and say it is a translation.
- 11 May, to Carnot: "La bataille de Lodi donne à la République toute l'Italie" (NO).
- The plaque in Lodi calls the French "invincible columns" (NO): useful as an example of how the event was remembered, not as description.

## Ground truth for the reconstruction
- The scenario (`src/data/lodi.json`, notes in `docs/lodi.md`) is built from the Wikipedia order of battle. It is an illustration, not a record: positions of individual units, the exact route of the cavalry and the moment-by-moment course of the fighting are **not known**. The documentary must say so on screen.
